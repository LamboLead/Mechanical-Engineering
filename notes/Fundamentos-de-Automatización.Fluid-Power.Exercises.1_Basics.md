The following are exercises from week 1 regarding fluid power laws and fundamentals of cylinders.

1. Three types of cylinder efficiency were presented. Why is important to know the cylinder efficiencies when selecting a cylinder for an application?

  **Ans:** The cylinder efficiencies allow to know the amount of power (overall efficiency), force (mechanical efficiency) or flow (volumetric efficiency) that the cylinder can deliver depending on its input variables and the construction of the cylinder. The output variable is optimized according to the application of the cylinder.

2. If a cylinder uses cup seals, explain why increasing the volumetric efficiency will likely reduce the mechanical efficiency, and vice-versa?

  **Ans:** By using cup seals, and increasing the fluid flow (thus increasing the velocity of the cylinder piston), the seal is pressed against the inner surface of the cylinder, thus increasing friction between those surfaces, and reducing the overall amount of force imparted by the piston, reducing the mechanical efficiency.
  
3. What is the force equation for an ideal cylinder?

  **Ans:** An ideal cylinder does not loose output force due to friction (i.e. its mechanical efficiency is equal to 1).

  $\eta_m = \frac{F_{out}}{P_{in}A} = 1 \space \Rightarrow F_{out} = P_{in}A$

4. What is the force equation for a real cylinder?

  **Ans:** A real cylinder takes into account losses due to friction, compressibility and other factors (i.e. its mechanical efficiency is $0 < \eta_m < 1$).

  $F_{out} = \eta_m \cdot P_{in}A$

5. What are the velocity equations for an ideal cylinder?
  
  **Ans:** An ideal cylinder does not loose fluid flow due to leakage, compressibility or other factors (i.e. its volumetric efficiency is equal to 1).

  $\eta_v = \frac{Av}{Q_{in}} = 1 \space \Rightarrow v = \frac{Q_{in}}{A}$

6. What are the velocity equations for a real cylinder?

  **Ans:** A real cylinder takes into account fluid losses due to leakage (i.e. its volumetric efficiency is $0 < \eta_v < 1$).

  $v = \eta_v \cdot \frac{Q_in}{A}$
